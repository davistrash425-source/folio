## B. US law

### B.1 FTC Act §5: privacy promises must match practice

The FTC treats a privacy policy statement that doesn't match what a company does as a deceptive practice, and the statute's general prohibition on unfair or deceptive acts is what it enforces (15 U.S.C. §45). The FTC's COPPA FAQ and the 2025 COPPA amendments reflect the same expectation that published retention and deletion practices be real ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions); [90 FR 16918](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)).

**For Folio:** every sentence has to be true of the code today. Statements that would be false now: "Your site and photos are then removed" (web copies can survive in browser caches for up to a year, reports and admin logs aren't deleted, database history keeps rows for a while); "After 3 upheld notices we close the account" (the code only prompts staff to suspend); "IP addresses are used briefly" (true for Upstash, but Turnstile and Vercel also get them); "They process data only to provide their service to Folio" (Cloudflare uses Turnstile signals to improve Turnstile, and Polar is an independent business). The rewritten pages fix each of these.

### B.2 Negative-option rules and auto-renewal laws

**FTC "click-to-cancel" rule: not in force.** The Eighth Circuit vacated the whole amended Negative Option Rule on July 8, 2025, days before compliance was due, in *Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC* ([Latham & Watkins alert](https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/Eighth-Circuit-Vacates-FTC-Click-to-Cancel-Rule-Days-Before-Compliance-Deadline.pdf), secondary; [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/7/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule), secondary). The FTC can still bring cases under ROSCA and §5, and the original 1973 rule still exists ([Dickinson Wright](https://www.dickinson-wright.com/news-alerts/rule-interrupted-click-to-cancel-is-click-to-gone), secondary). I found no reissued rule as of Nov 2026, but **this should be re-checked**.

**ROSCA** (15 U.S.C. §8403) still requires, for online negative-option sales, clear disclosure of material terms before billing, express informed consent, and simple cancellation. Not fetched in this session; flagged for the lawyer.

**California ARL** (Bus. & Prof. Code §17600 et seq., as amended by AB 2863 for contracts entered, amended or extended on or after July 1, 2025). From the bill text ([LegiScan AB 2863](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB2863/id/3012746), an amended version; **the chaptered text, Ch. 515 Stats. 2024, should be checked**) and summaries ([Latham](https://lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/In-California-New-Automatic-Renewal-and-Continuous-Service-Requirements-Signed-Into-Law.pdf), [Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/california-amends-automatic-renewal-law-again.html), secondary):
- Show the auto-renewal terms clearly and conspicuously **before** purchase, in **visual proximity** to the consent request (§17602(a)(1)).
- Get affirmative consent, and **express affirmative consent to the renewal terms** separately (§17602(a)(2), (a)(4)).
- **Keep proof of consent for 3 years, or 1 year after the contract ends, whichever is longer** (§17602(a)(6)).
- Send an **acknowledgment** with the terms, cancellation policy and how to cancel, in a form the customer can keep (§17602(a)(3)).
- Allow **online cancellation** at will without obstruction: a prominent link/button or a pre-written email (§17602(d)). A save offer is allowed if cancellation stays easy (§17602(e)).
- **Price change notice 7–30 days** before it takes effect, with how to cancel (§17602(g)(2)); notice of material changes (§17602(g)(1)).
- **Annual plans:** a reminder 15–45 days before renewal where the initial term is a year or more (§17602(b)(2)), and an **annual reminder** in the same medium (§17602(h)).

**Florida** §501.165 applies to "service contracts" with automatic renewal that keeps the contract running more than 6 months and renews for more than a month ([Fla. Stat. §501.165](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/501.165)). So **monthly Pro is probably outside it; yearly Pro is probably inside it**: clear disclosure; for an initial term of 12 months or more, written or electronic notice **30–60 days before the cancellation deadline**; cancellation by the same means used to sign up; a violation makes the renewal clause void. Whether a SaaS subscription is a "written contract … for a fixed period" is a question for the lawyer.

**For Folio:**
- Polar's buyer terms say Polar shows price, frequency, cancellation and recurring-charge disclosures at checkout and that cancellation is self-serve in the portal ([Polar Buyer Terms](https://polar.sh/legal/checkout-buyer-terms)). That likely covers much of the checkout side, but **Folio's own "Go Pro" buttons (`app/dashboard/manage/billing/buttons.tsx`) and `/pricing` say nothing about renewal** (gap G-03). Whether Polar sends renewal reminders for yearly plans (CA 15–45 days; FL 30–60 days) is **unknown**: check Polar's dashboard or ask Polar. If not, Folio needs its own reminder email (code task).
- Under the ARL, Folio is likely still "the business making the offer" even though Polar is the seller; a lawyer should say whether Polar's compliance covers Folio.

### B.3 CCPA/CPRA

A for-profit business doing business in California is covered if it has over **$26,625,000** gross revenue (2025–26 figure), buys/sells/shares personal information of **100,000+** California consumers or households, or gets **50%+** of revenue from selling/sharing ([CPPA announcement](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241217.html); [CPPA thresholds page](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html)). **Folio meets none today.** It doesn't sell or share data for cross-context advertising.

**Voluntary disclosure suggestion:** say Folio doesn't sell or share personal information for advertising, offer the same access/delete/correct rights to everyone, and don't claim to be "CCPA compliant". The CCPA's breach-damages provision (§1798.150) only applies to covered businesses, but California's separate breach-notice law (Civ. Code §1798.82) applies to anyone holding Californians' data (not fetched; flagged).

### B.4 Florida Digital Bill of Rights and Florida breach law

**FDBR** controllers must have **over $1 billion** global revenue plus an ads, smart-speaker or app-store test ([Wilmer Hale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/WilmerHale-Privacy-and-Cybersecurity-Law/20230515-florida-legislature-passes-privacy-law), secondary; [DWT tracker](https://www.dwt.com/gcp/state-privacy-laws-tracker/broad-state-privacy-law-tracker/florida), secondary). Folio isn't one. **But §501.715 bars any for-profit business from selling sensitive data without consent** ([Squire Patton Boggs via NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/florida-joins-privacy-pack-opt-to-sale-sensitive-data), secondary). Folio sells nothing, so a "we don't sell" statement covers it. (The official §501.702 page wouldn't load.)

**Breach notice, §501.171** ([Fla. Stat. §501.171](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/501.171)) applies to any commercial entity, sole proprietors included:
- "Personal information" includes **an email address plus a password** that would allow access to an online account. Folio stores scrypt hashes, and the statute excludes data that is encrypted or otherwise unusable; whether a salted scrypt hash counts is a lawyer question.
- Notify affected individuals **within 30 days** of determining a breach; notify the Florida Department of Legal Affairs within 30 days if **500+ Floridians** are affected; consumer reporting agencies if **1,000+** are notified.
- Vendors ("third-party agents") must tell Folio **within 10 days**.
- Requires "reasonable measures" to protect data and to dispose of records.
- No private right of action; enforced by the state, with civil penalties up to $500,000.

**For Folio:** write a one-page breach plan (what counts, who decides, the 30-day clock, templates). The Privacy Policy should promise only what that plan delivers: "we'll tell affected people without unreasonable delay and as the law requires". Other states have their own breach laws (all 50 do); not researched individually.

### B.5 COPPA and an 18+ rule

COPPA covers sites directed to children under 13, or general-audience sites with **actual knowledge** they're collecting personal information from a child under 13. Operators aren't required to investigate ages. A photo containing a child's image counts as a child's personal information, but **adults uploading photos of children to a general-audience service doesn't trigger COPPA**, which covers only information collected online from children ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions), Q A.2, A.3, F.4, H.1, H.3). The amended Rule took effect June 23, 2025, with compliance due April 22, 2026 ([90 FR 16918](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)).

If Folio asks for age, the FTC's guidance is to ask **neutrally** (e.g. a date of birth), not a pre-ticked or "I'm over 12" box, and to stop a user going back to change the answer (FAQ H.3). If Folio learns a user is under 13, it must delete their information or comply with COPPA (FAQ H.6).

**Smallest sign-up change that enforces 18+ (proposal; not built):** add "I'm 18 or older" to the existing Terms checkbox label ("I'm 18 or older and agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy") on both sign-up forms, and store the confirmation with `termsAcceptedAt`. That's a self-declaration, like ShootProof and Format, not neutral age screening. A neutral birth-date field is stronger evidence but adds friction and data; the lawyer should choose. Either way, add a support process: delete an account on learning the person is under 18 (under 13 is the legal minimum; 18 is Folio's contract choice, and minors' contracts can be voidable).

### B.6 Clickwrap and notice of changes

- Courts enforce online terms when the site gives **reasonably conspicuous notice** of the terms and the user takes an action that **unambiguously shows assent**, such as ticking a box saying "I agree" ([Berman v. Freedom Financial Network, 30 F.4th 849 (9th Cir. 2022)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/20-16900/20-16900-2022-04-05.html)). A button press alone counts only if the user is told that clicking means agreeing (per Berman, as applied in later Ninth Circuit memoranda such as [Cullors v. Cerebral](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2024/07/12/23-55594.pdf)). Hyperlinks styled to look like links (blue/underlined) helped in *Meyer v. Uber* (2d Cir. 2017), as described in [Severson](https://www.severson.com/?p=24316), secondary.
- **Folio's sign-up** uses an unticked checkbox, "I agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy", with underlined links opening in a new tab, and blocks sign-up without it (`app/ui/signup-form.tsx`, `signup` in `app/actions/auth.ts`). That's the strongest common pattern. Gaps: the time is stored but **not which version** was accepted; and Google sign-up has the same checkbox (good).
- **Changes:** posting new terms on the site **doesn't bind existing customers who weren't told** ([Douglas v. U.S. Dist. Ct., 495 F.3d 1062 (9th Cir. 2007)](https://www.jenner.com/a/web/iupRRz85ELL8PWsdaWicdC/4HRMZQ/Douglas_v_US_District_Court.pdf)). Retroactive changes to arbitration terms without notice contributed to unconscionability in *Heckman* (below). **For Folio:** email account holders about material changes in advance (suggest 30 days, matching Polar's own 30-day notice for adverse changes), don't apply changes to disputes that already exist, and for big changes (adding arbitration) ask for a fresh click.

### B.7 Arbitration, class waivers and liability caps

- **Arbitration:** enforceable in principle under the FAA, but mass-arbitration rules that are one-sided can make the whole clause unconscionable: *Heckman v. Live Nation*, 120 F.4th 670 (9th Cir. 2024) ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/10/ninth-circuits-live-nation-decision-evaluates-enforceability-of-mass-arbitration-protocols), [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/print/pdf/node/9203), secondary). Peers that arbitrate use JAMS/NAM/AAA, a pre-dispute notice period, a small-claims carve-out and often a 30-day opt-out (§A.1). **Trade-off for Folio:** arbitration protects against class actions (the main risk for auto-renewal claims) but has real costs: consumer arbitration fees usually fall on the business, and mass filings can cost more than litigation. Polar's own buyer terms already send payment disputes with Polar to JAMS/AAA arbitration with a 30-day opt-out ([Polar Buyer Terms](https://polar.sh/legal/checkout-buyer-terms)). **Lawyer's decision.** The draft gives it as a bracketed option.
- **Liability caps (Florida):** Florida enforces exculpatory clauses if the intent is clear and unequivocal and an ordinary person would understand what they're giving up, though such clauses are disfavoured: *Sanislo v. Give Kids the World*, 157 So. 3d 256 (Fla. 2015) ([White and Williams summary](https://whiteandwilliams.com/the-subrogation-strategist/in-florida-exculpatory-clauses-do-not-need-express-language-referring-to-the-exculpated-partys-negligence), secondary). So plain wording helps enforceability, and caps usually can't cover intentional misconduct or gross negligence. The draft follows peer practice (the greater of $50 or 12 months' fees) with carve-outs; the lawyer should pick the number.
- Consumers vs small businesses: most Folio users are sole-proprietor photographers, who may count as consumers in some states and as businesses in others (e.g. the Florida §501.165 "consumer" definition excludes business use). A lawyer should decide whether to treat everyone as a consumer (safer) or not.

### B.8 DMCA §512 check of `/copyright`

From [17 U.S.C. §512](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512):
- **§512(c)(1):** no knowledge (or act fast once aware), no direct financial benefit where you control the infringement, and remove quickly after a proper notice.
- **§512(c)(2):** designate an agent with the Copyright Office **and** post the agent's name, address, phone and email on the site. Folio: registered (DMCA-1084175) and shown via `DMCA_AGENT`. ✓
- **§512(c)(3):** the six notice elements; a notice that substantially gives (ii)–(iv) but not the rest obliges you to try to get a complete one. Folio's form collects all six. ✓ The page doesn't say what Folio does with an incomplete notice (minor).
- **§512(g)(2):** to keep the "good-faith takedown" protection: (A) promptly notify the user; (B) on a valid counter-notice, promptly send a copy to the complainant saying the material will be back in 10 business days; (C) **put it back no sooner than 10 and no later than 14 business days** after the counter-notice unless the complainant says they've sued. Folio's email to photographers (`lib/admin-emails.ts`) states this correctly. **The `/copyright` page says only "may restore … within the legal period"**: change "may" to "will" and give the 10–14 business days.
- **§512(g)(3):** counter-notice elements: signature, what was removed and where, a perjury statement of mistake/misidentification, name/address/phone, consent to federal court jurisdiction, and acceptance of service. **The page doesn't list them**; the email does. Add them to the page.
- **§512(i)(1)(A):** adopt, reasonably implement, and **inform users of** a repeat-infringer termination policy. Folio: the policy is stated (3 strikes), and strikes are tracked. But **the code doesn't close accounts automatically at 3**; it tells staff to suspend. "Reasonably implemented" is about practice, so the page should describe what staff actually do ("we suspend and then close") and staff must actually do it. The Cox cases show losing the safe harbor for not implementing a stated policy ([Daeryun summary](https://www.daeryunlaw.com/ru/practices/detail/dmca-compliance), secondary).
- **§512(m):** no duty to monitor. Fine to say so.
- **Not in the page:** that a sender who knowingly misrepresents can be liable (§512(f)). The notice form page already warns of this (`app/(auth)/report/copyright/page.tsx`); repeat it on `/copyright`.
- The agent registration must be renewed every 3 years (owner's note: by Oct 2029).

## C. International

### C.1 Does GDPR / UK GDPR apply?

**Art. 3(2)(a), offering services:** the EDPB says mere accessibility of a website, an email address, or using English isn't enough; factors include naming EU countries, EU currencies or languages, EU marketing, EU phone numbers or domains, and an international clientele ([EDPB Guidelines 3/2018](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_3_2018_territorial_scope_after_public_consultation_en_1.pdf), pp. 17–19). Folio: English only, USD only, US operator, `.cam` domain, no EU marketing. **On these facts, Folio probably isn't targeting the EU**. But an EU photographer who signs up and pays is being served, and if Folio markets in the EU later, that changes.

**Art. 3(2)(b), monitoring:** tracking people online, particularly with profiling, counts; purpose matters (pp. 19–20). Folio's analytics are cookieless aggregate counts on its own pages and its Sentry setup doesn't profile. **Probably not monitoring**.

**EU hosting doesn't by itself make Folio subject to GDPR.** The Guidelines say a non-EU controller doesn't become subject merely by using a processor in the EU (Section 1 of the Guidelines, not re-fetched; flagged). Vercel `fra1` and Neon Frankfurt are processors.

**Art. 27 representative:** required only if Art. 3(2) applies; waived for occasional, low-risk processing not involving large-scale special-category data (pp. 25–26). If the lawyer concludes GDPR applies, Folio's processing is regular, so the waiver probably doesn't fit, and a representative (a paid service, e.g. Pic-Time uses Prighter for the UK and a Vienna firm for the EU) would be needed.

**Light-touch but honest approach (recommended for the draft):** a short "If you're in the EU or UK" section listing lawful bases (contract for accounts; legitimate interests for security, rate limits and error logs; consent where asked), the rights, how transfers work (most data is in Germany; some vendors are in the US and rely on their own transfer terms), and the right to complain to a regulator, **without** saying "GDPR applies" or "we comply with GDPR". This mirrors the peers (§A.2) at lower cost.

### C.2 Cookies and ePrivacy

Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive covers storing or reading information on a user's device: cookies, but also tracking pixels, tracking links and JavaScript that instructs the browser to send information ([EDPB Guidelines 2/2023, final Oct 2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-22023-on-technical-scope-of-art-53-of-eprivacy-directive_en); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/edpb-publishes-guidelines-to-clarify-scope-of-eu-cookie-notice-and-consent-requirements), secondary). Consent isn't needed for what's strictly necessary for the service the user asked for (login, security). Some regulators (CNIL) exempt narrowly configured audience measurement ([summary](https://www.databuddy.cc/blog/is-cookieless-tracking-gdpr-compliant), secondary).

**For Folio:** session, gallery, manager, impersonation and Google-OAuth cookies are strictly necessary. Turnstile is security, normally treated as necessary. **Vercel Web Analytics** is cookieless, but its script sends data from the browser, so under the EDPB's reading it may be within Art. 5(3). It's aggregate, first-party and short-lived, which is the kind of setup some regulators exempt. **A banner isn't needed for US users; for EU/UK visitors it's arguable for analytics alone.** Options: (a) no banner, disclose analytics clearly (current approach); (b) don't load analytics for EU/UK visitors (Vercel gives country); (c) a banner. Lawyer's call (LAWYER-QUESTIONS Q10).

### C.3 Israel: Privacy Protection Law and Amendment 13

Amendment 13 to the Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981 took effect **August 14, 2025**: a controller/processor model, broader notice duties under §11, wider definitions, data protection officers for some organisations, narrowed database registration, and administrative fines from the Privacy Protection Authority ([Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/pc-regulatory-influencer-israel-modernises-privacy-law-comprehensive), [Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/?p=62239), [Chambers 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/israel), all secondary). The §11 notice must say whether giving the data is a legal requirement, the purposes, the controller, and more (Chambers).

**Territorial scope is the open question.** Secondary sources disagree: some say the law reaches foreign companies processing Israeli residents' data ([Safetica](https://www.safetica.com/resources/guides/israel-s-amendment-13-what-the-new-data-protection-law-means-for-your-business)); others are more cautious, tying it to databases in Israel ([Presencis](https://cdn.presencis.com/regulations/il-ppl/timeline/)). I couldn't find an English official text or PPA guidance on extraterritorial reach.

**Does the operator's temporary presence in Israel matter?** Possibly: if David manages the database (runs `/admin`, handles support) from Israel, an Israeli regulator could see a "database managed in Israel". That's a real question for an Israeli lawyer, not a US one. **Practical step:** the Privacy Policy's notice items (purposes, controller identity, whether data is mandatory, recipients, rights) cover §11's list, so the draft adds an "Israel" line and doesn't claim compliance.

## D. Platform and vendor obligations

### D.1 Google API Services User Data Policy

Apps must publish an accurate privacy policy about Google user data, use it only for prominent user-facing features, not transfer it except in listed cases, not let humans read it except with consent/for security/law/aggregated operations, and never use it for ads ([Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy)). The Limited Use rules formally attach to sensitive and restricted scopes. `drive.file` is a non-sensitive scope (HANDOFF notes the consent screen setup), and `openid email profile` are basic sign-in scopes.

Many apps post this statement (Google's standard Limited Use wording; I couldn't confirm from Google's page whether it's required for Folio's scopes, e.g. [Rollout](https://rollout.com/docs/security-legal/google-api-services-disclosure)): *Folio's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.* **Recommendation:** include it anyway. It's free, true of Folio's code (Drive data never reaches Folio's server; sign-in data is used only to sign in), and future-proofs a scope change. Also say plainly what Folio gets from Google and what it doesn't.

### D.2 Polar (merchant of record)

From Polar's [Master Services Terms](https://polar.sh/legal/master-services-terms) and [Buyer Terms](https://polar.sh/legal/checkout-buyer-terms):
- **Polar is the reseller and the seller to the buyer** (MST 4.1, 10.1). Folio must not invoice buyers directly (10.1), and its pages must make Polar's reseller status clear (9.2); Polar may require changes (9.3).
- **Refund eligibility must be disclosed** in the product information and on Folio's pages (9.5). Polar decides refunds "in its sole discretion" (10.2) and may refund to prevent chargebacks; Folio repays refunds and chargebacks plus fees (10.5).
- **If the product becomes unavailable, Folio must keep access through the paid period or give pro-rated refunds** (6.4). That bears on Folio terminating a paying account, and on account deletion revoking immediately.
- Folio must respond to Polar's information requests within 72 hours (6.2) and report unauthorised access to buyer data within 72 hours (15.4).
- **Data roles:** each party is an independent "business" under US state privacy laws, not a service provider (15.5). So the Privacy Policy should present Polar as **a separate company with its own privacy policy**, not as Folio's processor.
- **Buyer terms:** subscriptions renew until cancelled; cancellation is effective at the next billing cycle; fees for billed periods generally aren't refundable unless the law requires; Polar gives advance notice of price increases; Delaware law and arbitration govern the buyer–Polar relationship ([Buyer Terms](https://polar.sh/legal/checkout-buyer-terms)).

**What Folio's Terms must/mustn't say:** name Polar as the seller and merchant of record; point to Polar's buyer terms for payment, tax and refunds; state Folio's refund position consistently with Polar's (don't promise refunds Folio can't make itself, since only Polar issues them; don't say "no refunds ever", since Polar may refund anyway and the law may require it); keep the subscription description (what Pro includes, renewal, cancellation) accurate.

### D.3 Cloudflare Turnstile

Turnstile collects IP, TLS fingerprint, user agent and site key/origin; Cloudflare is a **processor** for the bot check but a **controller** when it uses those signals to improve Turnstile; it says the signals are strictly necessary; it gives **no retention period**; it doesn't require operators to mention it, but routes data-subject requests for processor activity to the operator ([Turnstile Privacy Addendum](https://www.cloudflare.com/turnstile-privacy-policy/)). **For Folio:** name Turnstile, link the addendum, and stop saying vendors use data "only to provide their service to Folio".

### D.4 Vercel Web Analytics

No cookies; visitors are identified by a hash of the request, discarded after 24 hours; each data point can include time, URL and route, referrer, filtered query parameters, country/region/city, OS, browser and device type; Vercel says data is aggregated and not tied to an individual or IP; it recommends `beforeSend` to strip personal data from URLs ([Vercel Analytics privacy](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/privacy-policy)). **For Folio:** the root domain hosts `/reset-password?token=…` and `/verify-email?token=…`; Vercel says query params are "filtered" but doesn't define it, so strip the query string in `beforeSend` (code task).

### D.5 Other vendor facts used

- **Vercel runtime logs:** Pro keeps 1 day; 30 days with Observability Plus ([Vercel Observability Plus](https://vercel.com/docs/observability/observability-plus)).
- **Neon restore history:** Free 6 h; Launch default 1 day, up to 7; Scale default 1 day, up to 30 ([Neon history window](https://neon.com/docs/postgres/backup-restore/history-window)).
- **Resend:** "30-day data retention" on Free and Pro per a copy of its pricing page ([Resend pricing mirror](https://resend-1aq0vtnut-resend.vercel.app/pricing.md); an independent review disagrees with 1–3 days, [IconPolls](https://iconpolls.com/blogs/resend-review-2026-email-api-sign-up-login-free-plan-pricing-user-experience-and-faqs)). **Check in the Resend dashboard.** Turning off content storage is a paid add-on with eligibility rules ([Resend KB](https://www.resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/how-do-i-ensure-sensitive-data-isnt-stored-on-resend)).
- **Sentry:** IP storage can be turned off with the project setting "Prevent Storing of IP Addresses" ([Sentry docs](https://gh.hhj520.top/getsentry/sentry-docs/pull/17986/files), from the docs source; secondary copy). Retention by plan (30 days on the free Developer plan, 90 on Team) is from a secondary list and should be checked in Sentry's billing page.

## E. Photography-specific issues

- **Model releases and consent.** No statute makes the hosting platform get releases; platforms put the duty on the uploader. ShootProof requires releases from everyone pictured and written guardian consent for identifiable minors; Format requires permission to use third parties' images or names; Pic-Time makes the photographer warrant rights and indemnify privacy/publicity claims; Squarespace requires users to hold publicity rights (§A.1).
- **Right of publicity.** Florida bars publishing a person's photograph or likeness **for trade, commercial or advertising purposes** without express written or oral consent; parents or guardians consent for minors; there are exceptions for news and for a photo of someone solely as a member of the public who isn't named ([Fla. Stat. ch. 540 (2026)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/Chapter540/All); [Fla. Stat. §540.08 (2021)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2021/540.08)). California (Civ. Code §3344) and New York (Civ. Rights Law §§50–51) have similar laws (not fetched). A photographer's **portfolio** is self-promotion, so it may count as "advertising purposes" for identifiable people. That's why the Terms should require releases where the law needs them, rather than promise Folio checks.
- **Photos of minors.** COPPA isn't triggered by adults posting photos of children (§B.5), but minors' images carry reputational and safety risk. Recommend: releases signed by a parent/guardian for identifiable minors; a ban on sexualised images of minors (already in the Terms); a fast takedown path for a parent's request. The web copies already have GPS removed (`lib/images.ts`), which is a genuine safety point worth stating.
- **Client gallery privacy.** Peers say galleries are "private" but none says the image files are publicly addressable. Folio's galleries: random 48-bit link (`newGallerySlug`), password (≥6 characters), 30-day access cookie, rate-limited unlocking, `/g/` disallowed in robots.txt, but the photos are the public web copies with long unguessable addresses. Recommend stating exactly that: "keeps the gallery out of sight; anyone who has an image's address can open that image". And tell photographers not to use galleries for photos that must stay confidential (e.g. boudoir), or to get the client's agreement.
